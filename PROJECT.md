Everything needed to pick this up cold. Written 26 August 2026.

## What it is

A small web game made as an **18th birthday present** for Robis's girlfriend,
whom he calls **Pookie**. She is in **Latvia**; he is **abroad**. She opens it
alone on her iPhone.

You drag a small green slime through a landscape made out of their real story.
Along the way are **stations** — a photograph, a letter, a gift, a fire, a
cairn — and tapping one opens what he wrote about that moment. The walk ends at
**eighteen things I love about you**.

It installs to the iPhone home screen from Safari and runs offline.

**Live:** https://us-fawn-phi.vercel.app
**Repo:** https://github.com/robisnik/us (private)

## The story

29 moments, chronological, drawn from prose he wrote in Pages and pasted in.
Source of truth: `content/story/our-story.md`. **His words are never edited** —
only split into moments and tagged with which station holds each.

The arc: Snapchat on 6 September → noticing her at a school Christmas party →
the Vienna choir trip → first real talk → the Esplanade → holding hands at the
cinema → the windowsill → Klusais centrs and Ziedoņdārzs → Jūrmala → his
birthday → Poland → driver's licence → **three days at her father's lake, where
he said he loved her** → meeting parents → packing → the last dinner at
32. Augusts restaurant → the airport → FaceTime every day since → **eighteen
things**.

Station distribution: 6 photo, 6 cairn (milestones), 5 cups (rituals),
5 bloom (gifts), 3 pin (places), 2 fire (together), 1 hourglass (the airport,
where the story bends), 1 letter (the eighteen).

### Authoring format

Blank line ends a moment. Everything else is an optional hint:

    [photo] [letter] [gift] [milestone] [place] [song] [ritual] [together]
    [idea] [wait] [growing] [postbox] [intro]
    [when: november 2019]
    [photo: riga-bar.jpg]
    [title: the night we met]

- `[intro]` is the opening screen, not a station. Its line breaks are kept.
- A title the parser derives from his opening words is marked `auto_title` and
  is **not** shown in the card, where it would sit above the line it came from.
- Prose is unwrapped (hard line breaks joined) so it reflows to any screen —
  except lines starting a list item, which is why the eighteen stay eighteen.
- An unrecognised tag prints a warning rather than silently becoming prose.

### Photos — drop-in

Put files in `content/photos/` and reference them as `[photo: name.jpg]`.
`build_story.py` resolves the extension automatically (`.jpg .jpeg .png .webp
.heic`). Missing files are noted and the moment keeps an empty frame, so the
app never breaks waiting for them. **Photos are gitignored** and are not
deployed by default — they were always intended to move to Supabase Storage
(private bucket, signed URLs). Google Drive was considered and rejected: no
stable direct-image URL, so images would fail intermittently on her phone.

## How it moves

Three things, all load-bearing:

1. **Fixed simulation step** (1/120). Physics never sees a variable `dt`, so a
   spring behaves identically at 60Hz and 120Hz.
2. **Interpolated rendering.** The renderer draws *between* the last two
   simulated states using the leftover time. Without this a fixed step judders.
3. **Nothing snaps.** No rounded positions, no velocity cut-off at a threshold
   — each is a single-frame discontinuity and the eye reads every one.

The slime is a ring of 26 points on springs that drag on their neighbours, so
it squashes into a shove and jiggles back. Deformation is driven by
**acceleration**, so it squashes when starting and stopping and relaxes while
gliding. Hard clamps on drive, offset and velocity, and the spring runs at its
own fixed 1/240 substep — an earlier version was driven by `pushX / dt` with no
ceiling and a fast flick threw the rim across the screen.

### The land

`terrain.js`. Height is a pure function of x — three sines at unrelated
periods, so it is identical on every device and every visit and costs no
memory. Seven **regions** each own a stretch with their own palette, ground
shape and scenery, and they crossfade over 420px so there is no visible
boundary:

    school → vienna → riga → jurmala → lake → goodbye → now

The creature is held to the ground rather than floating over it; climbing costs
a little speed and descending gives it back. The camera follows the climb more
slowly than the walk, so cresting a hill reveals what is beyond it.

### Security model

Signing up gets you an account and **nothing else**. Access needs a row in
`members`, which only the signup trigger creates, and only for an email already
listed in `invited`. A stranger who signs up sees an **empty app**, not an
error — an error would tell them something is there.

Verified from outside: anonymous reads return `[]`; anonymous writes to `notes`
and to `invited` (the door) are both rejected.

Tables: `invited`, `members`, `notes` (letters/replies/postbox — one shape, a
`kind` column), `visits`. Private storage bucket `photos`.

**Whether the bucket was actually created is unconfirmed** — to an anonymous
caller a private bucket and a missing one are indistinguishable. `invite.sql`
prints the answer; nobody has read the output yet.

Sign-in is a **magic link**: no password is ever created, typed or stored. The
tokens arrive in the URL fragment and are stripped from the address bar.

The **publishable key is in `backend.js` and committed** — it ships in the
deployed JavaScript regardless, so hiding it would be theatre. It grants
nothing without RLS passing. The `service_role` key is not in this repo and
must never be; schema changes are done by pasting SQL into the dashboard so
that key never leaves his browser.

`invited` currently contains **only his address**. Pookie's line is commented
out in `invite.sql`.

## Things that cost time — don't rediscover them

- **Service workers fail over HTTP/1.0** with "unknown error when fetching the
  script". The stdlib server defaults to 1.0; `scripts/serve.py` forces 1.1.
- **`apple-touch-icon` must be a PNG.** An SVG is silently ignored and iOS uses
  a screenshot of the page instead.
- **New Vercel projects are private by default** (`ssoProtection`), 302-ing
  every request to a login page. A service worker cannot register behind that,
  so the app is not installable until it is turned off.
- **The `var()` fallback trap:** an unresolved custom property inside
  `color-mix()` computes to the property's *initial* value, not the declaration
  above it. The fallback must live inside `var()`.
- **macOS keychain keys git credentials by host + username.** Without the
  username in the remote URL, two GitHub accounts fight over one entry.
- **The embedded browser pane blocks service worker registration** — `fetch`
  works, `register()` does not. Only testable on a real origin.
- **The iOS Simulator needs a full Xcode install**, not just command-line
  tools.
- Pages files are compressed binary; text is not recoverable cleanly. Copy and
  paste instead of trying to parse them.
